What to do when you can't get anything done
Or why it's so hard to focus, especially when you really need to

Why is it so hard to focus?

Multitasking

It's especially hard to focus when there are many tasks and all of them are important. So where is that legendary multitasking ability when you really need it — and when every hiring manager seems to expect it? The truth is, it simply doesn't exist. Researchers found that the brain struggles to concentrate on even two tasks at the same time. When several important tasks compete for our attention, the body panics and releases cortisol and adrenaline — the so-called stress hormones. As a result, we work less carefully, make mistakes, and get tired quickly.

Dopamine

Stress hormones are one part of the story, but it gets even more interesting. Our inability to focus also involves something that seems like the complete opposite of stress: dopamine. This chemical plays an important role in the brain's reward system. What does that have to do with concentration? We often interrupt an important task to do something more enjoyable for the brain, such as checking likes on social media. At that moment, dopamine is released and we experience pleasure. This creates a vicious cycle: the more we get distracted, the more rewarding distraction can feel.

What reduces our ability to focus?

Multitasking

How can concentration decrease because of... concentration? Any stressful situation — and a rapidly changing world in general — makes our brain constantly scan the environment for potential danger. For example, we may start reading the news instead of working. But staying alert all the time is impossible. Concentration is like a muscle, and it can become exhausted if we don't give it time to rest.

Food

Fast carbohydrates — sugar, white bread, and sweets — quickly deliver energy to the brain and can temporarily help it work better. But that energy disappears just as quickly as it arrives. As a result, we end up on a blood sugar roller coaster: once the effect of fast carbohydrates wears off, we feel tired and sleepy and start craving even more of them.

Devices

Yes, it's that familiar situation: there's code on the laptop screen, a podcast playing through your headphones, and a video of funny ducklings running in the background. The brain keeps trying to switch from one task to another and eventually struggles to handle any of them properly. On average, it can take around 20 minutes to return to a state of full concentration. So the more time we spend being distracted by our devices, the more time we may need to get back to work.

How to focus better than a goldfish (that is, for more than three seconds)

5 simple (well, not really) tips

Imagine the sky and clouds

Or leaves floating down a stream. This is a simple form of meditation that can help you calm down. Anxious thoughts about difficult tasks are often exactly what prevents us from doing those tasks. At moments like this, you can close your eyes and imagine that you are the clear sky and your anxious thoughts are clouds. No matter how many clouds appear, the clear sky is always somewhere behind them and never disappears. This exercise can help bring you back to the present moment instead of worrying about potential problems in the future.

Put on some music

But not just any music — and preferably not your favorite songs. Music you love may be associated with strong positive emotions, which can also be distracting. Quiet, calm music with a moderate rhythm works better. You can also try a playlist with white noise or music designed to support concentration.

Take a walk

Or even go for a run. A park or forest is an especially good choice: one study found that looking at natural environments can improve concentration, while viewing urban environments may have the opposite effect. You can combine your walk with exercise or running to increase blood flow and oxygen supply to the brain. But even a simple twenty-minute walk is a great option.

Eat well

We're not your grandmother, but it really does matter. Choose foods that help prevent sudden spikes in blood sugar. Foods high in fiber are a good option: vegetables, berries, legumes such as chickpeas, lentils and beans, brown rice, and whole-grain bread.

Read

Real paper books. They really can help train concentration. Reading a social media post takes only a few minutes or even seconds, while an interesting book can hold your attention for hours and is a great way to practice sustained focus.
